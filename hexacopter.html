Toolchanger v3 (not my design)

Design requirements:

  1. Push balls to lock the tool and drone couplers together.
  2. Connect a barrel jack for data transfer.

This system has several problems:

  • Unnecessarily high complexity
  • Very difficult to assemble
  • Heavy
  • Little tolerance for error when aligning the couplers
  • Needs three pins for a higher data rate
  • Susceptible to impacts and vibration

Fourth iteration (my design)

Objective: redesign the system to fix the weaknesses of the prior version.

Error adjustment

  • The square couplers absorb rotational error.
  • Contact forces at the edges align the outer coupler to the inner one.
  • The funnel shape of the couplers absorbs translational error.

Two-motor assembly

  • Three points are needed to define a plane and keep the platform level.
  • Two motors alone are not enough: the platform can tilt along the axis between them.
  • Curved walls keep the platform from tilting more than a degree.
  • The three plane-defining points are the two motors and the contact walls.

Three barrel jacks

TPU cone nuts (brown) screw down between walls and squeeze the barrel jacks (bronze) to hold them in place.

No magnets

The differently shaped platform forces the locking balls into place and lets them fall back passively when unlocking, so magnets are no longer needed. Tolerances still need adjusting.

Vibration study: motor mount

The parts are modeled as isotropic. The bolts are locally bonded to the parts and the clamping face is a fixed geometry. The motors spin at about 14,000 rpm at hover, so I ran the study around the 250 Hz range. The first four modes are shown below.

The first two modes sit closest to the propeller operating range, so I redesigned the mount around them.
